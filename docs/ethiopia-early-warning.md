# Ethiopia Childhood Disease Early Warning

**Exploring how machine learning and environmental data can help anticipate changes in childhood disease burden.**

![Project](https://img.shields.io/badge/project-research_overview-4B5563)
![Source](https://img.shields.io/badge/source-private-111827)

Many public-health responses begin after a rise in illness is already visible in reported cases. This research explores whether patterns in health, weather, environmental, and population data could help identify changes in disease burden earlier, giving health systems more time to plan.

The work focuses on **pneumonia, malaria, and diarrheal disease among children under five in Ethiopia**.

## What I'm working on

- **Data engineering:** organizing heterogeneous health, geographic, environmental, and demographic information into consistent datasets for analysis.
- **Geospatial analysis:** aligning information across different geographic and temporal scales.
- **Predictive modeling:** developing and comparing statistical and machine-learning approaches to short-term disease forecasting.
- **Research validation:** studying data quality, missingness, and appropriate ways to evaluate forecasts over time.

The central question is not simply whether a model can fit historical data. It is whether a forecasting approach can be evaluated rigorously enough to become useful for public-health planning.

## Why it matters

Earlier and more reliable signals could eventually support decisions about staffing, medical supplies, and where to focus attention. Making those signals trustworthy requires careful handling of imperfect data, uncertainty, and changes in reporting patterns.

## Research principles

- **Real-world relevance:** work backward from public-health decisions rather than optimizing a metric in isolation.
- **Reproducibility:** keep data preparation, modeling, and evaluation organized and auditable.
- **Responsible evaluation:** distinguish development experiments from evidence of real-world predictive performance.
- **Partner stewardship:** respect research collaboration, data governance, and disclosure boundaries.

## About this repository

This is a **public project overview only**. The underlying research repository, data, implementation, model artifacts, and internal documentation are **not public**. This page does not contain runnable code or confidential partner information.

Some development and pipeline testing uses **synthetic or simulated disease outcomes**. No synthetic results should be interpreted as actual Ethiopian disease trends or evidence of operational forecasting performance.

**Current status:** active research and development; **not a deployed clinical or public-health alert system**. This is a personal description of research work, not an official publication, recommendation, or endorsement by a public-health institution or government partner.
